# Votes we deliberately do NOT score

Showing what we left out is part of the methodology. A scorecard that counts
every rejected amendment as a black mark turns nearly every member red, loses
all discriminating power, and is trivially dismissed as unfair. These are the
surveillance-related roll calls we found and chose not to score, with reasons.

They may still be displayed as context on a member's page. They never affect a
grade.

---

### 118th Senate, roll call 147 (Voteview 499) — 2024-04-19, 11–81
*"To prohibit the use of authorities under FISA to surveil United States persons,
to prohibit queries under such Act using U.S. person identifiers..."*

Effectively an end to Section 702 as it operates. It drew 11 votes. Treating a
NAY here as a pro-surveillance mark would tag 81 senators — including most of
the ones who voted for the warrant requirement an hour later — off a single
maximalist proposal. We score the warrant amendment (roll 148) instead, which
tests the same principle and is the vote civil-liberties groups actually
organized around.

### 118th Senate, roll call 145 (Voteview 497) — 2024-04-19, 17–75
*"To strike the prohibition on political appointees being involved in the
approval of queries by the FBI."*

Direction is genuinely ambiguous. Striking the provision was argued both as
removing a safeguard and as removing a poison pill that would have hampered
oversight. We will not score a vote whose direction we cannot state plainly.

### 118th Senate, roll call 146 (Voteview 498) — 2024-04-19, 34–58
*"To strike section 25, relating to definition of electronic communication
service provider."*

Substantively anti-surveillance — section 25 was the provision critics said
swept in ordinary businesses. Not scored in v1 only because it requires
explaining a double negative (a YEA strikes an expansion) to be understood, and
an unexplained badge is worse than no badge. Candidate for inclusion once the
measure page can carry the full explanation.

### 118th Senate roll calls 141, 143 (Voteview 493, 495) — cloture and motion to proceed
Procedural. Senators routinely vote for cloture on bills they then vote against,
and against cloture for reasons unrelated to the merits. Scoring procedure as
substance is the most common way scorecards get discredited.

### 118th House roll calls 115–118 (Voteview 833–836) — 2024-04-12
Amendments to H.R. 7888 that Voteview carries with no description. We will not
score a vote we cannot describe to a reader. Pending retrieval of amendment text
from the Congressional Record.

### 118th House roll call 120 (Voteview 838) — Table motion to reconsider
Pure procedure.

### 119th House roll calls 122, 124 (Voteview 482, 484) — HRES 1175 rules votes
Votes on the *rule* providing for consideration of H.R. 8035, not on FISA
itself. Rule votes are near-perfect party-line proxies and say nothing about a
member's position on surveillance.

### "Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act" (H.R. 5403, H.R. 1919)
Despite the name, this concerns central bank digital currency, not law
enforcement surveillance. Excluded as out of scope — including it because the
title contains "surveillance" would be exactly the kind of keyword-matching
error that discredits a database.
